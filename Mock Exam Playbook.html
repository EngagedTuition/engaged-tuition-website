Read the command word first

[most common mark loss]

Examiners mark against what the question asks for. Answering "describe" when it says "explain" can cap you at half marks, even if everything you wrote is correct.

Command wordWhat it meansWhat to write
State / GiveA short factOne word or line. Don't over-explain.
DescribeSay what happensTrends, features, steps. Quote data if given.
ExplainSay why it happensUse "because…", "this leads to…", "therefore…".
EvaluateWeigh it up and judgeBoth sides, "it depends on…", then a clear conclusion.
CompareSimilarities and differencesLink the two in each sentence ("whereas…").
CalculateWork out a numberEquation, substitution, answer, units. Method marks count even if the answer is wrong.

The four-week countdown

[plan]
  1. 4 weeks out

    List every topic and rate it

    Red, amber or green for each topic on the specification. Spend two-thirds of your time on red and amber.

  2. 3 weeks out

    Switch from notes to questions

    Re-reading feels productive but doesn't stick. Try "blurting": write everything you remember about a topic in 5 minutes, then fill the gaps in a different colour.

  3. 2 weeks out

    Do full past papers, timed

    Mark them with the official mark scheme from your exam board's website. Note the type of mark you lose: timing, command words, or content.

  4. Final week

    Fix the patterns, not everything

    Go back over the mistakes you keep repeating. Sleep counts as revision; late-night cramming before a paper usually costs more than it gains.

  5. In the exam

    Marks per minute

    Roughly a minute per mark. If a 2-mark question has taken 5 minutes, move on and come back to it.

For parents: how to help

[no nagging required]
  • Ask "what did you get wrong?" rather than "have you revised?". It gets a real conversation going.
  • Help them test themselves. Reading out flashcards for ten minutes helps more than supervising an hour of note-copying.
  • Treat mocks as a diagnosis. A disappointing mock grade tells you what to fix, with months still to go before the real exams.
  • Protect sleep and breaks. A tired student retains far less.
  • Get support early. A tutor is most useful straight after mocks, while there is still time to change the result.
